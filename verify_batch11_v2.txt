=== SLIDE 3 ===
  Today's calorie-tracking apps give you one number for an entire meal. For a mixed Indian plate — rice, dal, chapati, sabzi — that single number is wrong by design, because every item has a different calorie density.

We are building a system that changes this. From a single meal photo, it:
• Separates the plate into individual food items
• Estimates how many grams of each item are on the plate
• Looks up verified nutrition data from India's official food tables (IFCT 2017) and USDA
• Reports calories and macros per item, plus a confidence score so you know what's reliable

Key difference: the AI only identifies food — it never guesses calories. All numbers come from verified databases, calculated in plain code. When the system isn't sure, it asks you a quick question instead of guessing. Built for Indian meals first, runs on a regular laptop.

=== SLIDE 4 ===
  A mixed meal is not a single dish — but today's apps treat it like one.

THE PROBLEM
Photo logging is fast and convenient. But when you snap a thali, the app collapses 3–8 different foods into one guessed entry. A single serving-size mistake can throw off the calorie count by 50% or more.

WHY IT'S HARD
• No depth in a 2D photo — we can't see how tall the dal is
• Foods hide under each other (chicken under rice)
• The same food has wildly different calories depending on how it's cooked (cooked rice: 130 kcal/100g | raw rice: 345 kcal/100g)
• Indian dishes and regional names (pappu, koora, annam) aren't in global databases

WHAT WE'RE BUILDING
A system that sees the plate the way a nutritionist would — component by component — and grounds every number in a verified food-composition table. No hallucinated calories. Honest uncertainty. Runs offline on your laptop.

=== SLIDE 11 ===
  THE PROBLEM
Calorie-tracking apps collapse a mixed meal into one dish and one guessed number. For an Indian thali (rice, dal, chapati, 2–3 curries), that single number hides large errors, can't be verified, and gives no hint of how much to trust it.

WHAT WE'RE BUILDING
A system that takes one meal photo and produces a component-level nutrition report — not a single guess.

INPUT: One smartphone photo of a mixed Indian meal (3–8 items), plus optional notes like plate size or meal type. Text or voice input supported.

OUTPUT: For each food item — name, estimated grams (with range), calories, protein, carbs, fat, confidence score, and the database source. Plus a meal total with an overall reliability indicator.

HOW WE MEASURE SUCCESS
• Component identification accuracy (F1 score)
• Portion estimation error in grams
• Calorie error vs. weighed ground truth
• Calibration: does 80% confidence mean 80% accurate?
• Speed: under 20 seconds on a regular laptop

NON-NEGOTIABLE: This is an informational self-tracking tool — not medical, clinical, or diagnostic advice. Every result carries this disclaimer.

=== SLIDE 13 ===
  THE 9-STAGE PIPELINE: FROM PHOTO TO VERIFIED REPORT

1. INPUT & CONTEXT
   Photo uploaded; optional plate size, meal type, or diet goals noted.

2. QUALITY CHECK & ROUTING
   Blur/exposure validated; simple vs. complex plate routed to appropriate depth.

3. SEE THE COMPONENTS (Perception)
   Vision model finds each food item, draws its region, labels its state (cooked/raw/fried/gravy). CRITICAL: The model NEVER outputs calories or grams.

4. LOOK UP VERIFIED NUTRITION (RAG)
   Hybrid search matches each item to IFCT 2017 / USDA. Physical-state filter ensures cooked rice matches cooked rice (130 kcal), not raw (345 kcal).

5. ESTIMATE PORTION MASS
   Plate diameter → pixel scale → food area → heap volume × density → grams with an 80% confidence interval.

6. CALCULATE NUTRITION
   Pure Python: (nutrient per 100g × grams ÷ 100) for each item. Sum for meal total.

7. SANITY CHECK (Critic)
   Automatic veto on impossible portions or macro ratios before you see results.

8. CALIBRATED CONFIDENCE
   7 pipeline signals fused → isotonic regression → honest reliability score.

9. DECIDE & CLARIFY
   High confidence? Deliver report. Low confidence? Ask 1–2 targeted questions, recalculate only what's affected.

10. PERSIST EVERYTHING
   Full audit trail: model version, prompt version, KB version, compute version — deterministic replay guaranteed.

=== SLIDE 14 ===
  FOUR CORE MECHANISMS THAT MAKE IT WORK

1. VISION THAT DOESN'T HALLUCINATE
   The AI is constrained by a strict output schema: label, bounding box, physical state, relative size. ZERO numeric fields. It literally cannot output a calorie number.

2. RETRIEVAL THAT KNOWS RAW FROM COOKED
   Dense + keyword search over IFCT 2017 & USDA, fused with reciprocal rank fusion. A physical-state filter rejects mismatches — cooked rice never matches raw rice. Hard cutoff at 0.55 similarity: below that, we say 'uncertain,' not 'here's a guess.'

3. PORTIONS FROM GEOMETRY, NOT GUESSES
   Plate diameter in the photo gives us pixels-per-cm. Segmented food area × category-specific heap height × food density = grams. Log-normal confidence interval reflects real uncertainty.

4. CONFIDENCE THAT'S STATISTICALLY HONEST
   7 observable signals (identity match, retrieval score, portion plausibility, critic verdict, image quality, state certainty, visibility) → weighted fusion → isotonic calibration on held-out weighed meals. The result: when we say 80% confidence, we're right ~80% of the time.

WHY STATE FILTERING MATTERS:
Cooked white rice = 130 kcal/100g | Raw rice = 345 kcal/100g. Without the filter, a basic staple is overestimated by 2.6×. This is the single largest systematic error in the field — and we eliminate it by design.

  WHAT THE SYSTEM MUST DO (Functional)

• Break a mixed plate into 3–8 items with regions and prep states
• Estimate grams per item using the plate as a scale reference
• Match each item to IFCT 2017 / USDA with raw/cooked filtering
• Calculate nutrition in pure Python — missing values stay missing
• Sanity-check portions and macros before showing results
• Report a calibrated confidence score you can trust
• Ask at most 2 questions, only when your answer changes the result
• Save full audit trail for exact reproducibility

QUALITY & CONSTRAINTS (Non-Functional)

• Accuracy: Component F1 ≥ 0.80 | Calorie MAPE ≤ 18% | Calibration error ≤ 0.10
• Speed: Median ≤ 8 sec, 95th percentile ≤ 20 sec on complex plates
• Cost: ≤ $0.01 per analysis
• Offline-first: Full pipeline runs without internet (MOCK_MODE)
• Zero hallucination: Every number traces to a database row
• Runs on: Standard Windows laptop, 8 GB RAM, CPU only
• Privacy: Photos and data stay local; user controls deletion
• Safety: Non-medical disclaimer on every result
